## Course overview

TECH 65000 introduces statistical inference and probabilistic discriminative machine learning. Probabilistic graphical models provide a common language for representing model structure, conditional independence, and factorization.

The course begins with mathematical and computational foundations: linear algebra, derivatives, Python, probability rules, random variables, common distributions, and graphical models. It then develops likelihood, maximum-likelihood estimation, and the connection between probabilistic assumptions and familiar loss functions such as squared error and cross-entropy.

Classical statistical inference is connected to modern machine-learning practice through uncertainty quantification, experimental reasoning, model selection, and generalization. Later topics include exponential-family distributions, Bayesian inference, regression and classification models, numerical optimization, and an introduction to neural networks.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

- analyze how data and statistical methods support organizational decision-making;
- apply appropriate descriptive and inferential techniques to complex business and industrial problems;
- evaluate the validity and reliability of statistical findings in academic and professional research;
- use statistical software to design, conduct, and interpret quantitative analyses; and
- communicate statistical results clearly to technical and non-technical audiences.
